就是腿折了 (jiù shì tuǐ zhé le) is a Chinese phrase that literally translates to "it's just that my legs 
are broken." However, the context and usage can be quite different from its literal meaning. Here’s how you 
might interpret it:

### Literal Meaning:
- **就是** (jiù shì): means "just," indicating something simple or straightforward.
- **腿折了** (tuǐ zhé le): means "my legs are broken."

So, literally put together, "it's just that my legs are broken" would imply a physical injury.

### Figurative Meaning:
In colloquial Chinese usage, this phrase is often used to downplay or minimize something. It can be seen as 
a way of saying:

- **It’s nothing serious**: *我这就过来了，就是腿折了* (Wǒ zhè jiù guò lái le, jiù shì tuǐ zhé le) — "I’m 
coming over now; it's just that my legs are broken."
  
  In this context, the speaker might be using an exaggerated phrase to make a situation seem less severe or 
more trivial.

- **Just something minor**: *这件事就只是腿折了* (Zhège shì jing jiù zhǐ shì tuǐ zhé le) — "This thing is 
just that my legs are broken."

  Here, the speaker might be minimizing an issue by using this phrase to indicate it’s not a big deal.

### Example Usage in Context:
- **客户问你为什么迟到** (Kè hú wèn nǐ wèi shén me chídào) — "The customer asks you why you are late."
  - **你可以说：就是腿折了，我在路上崴了一下。** (Nǐ kě yuē: jiù shì tuǐ zhé le, wǒ zài lù shàng wǎi zhe yī 
xià.) — "It's just that my legs are broken; I twisted them while walking on the road."
